## 1. RSI-Prime: Training Models to Train Models

**Authors:** Dewu Zheng, Yang Ye, Hongyu Zhang, Jun Yu, Yanlin Wang
**arXiv:** [2611.23138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138)
**Categories:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

Enabling large language models (LLMs) to autonomously conduct training and improve target models is an important foundation for recursive self-improvement (RSI). As LLMs expand their role from being trained to conducting training, their model-training capability warrants dedicated study and systematic development. To this end, we construct RSI-Prime, a dataset for improving model-training capability, comprising 350 complete experimental trajectories across seven task categories. To help LLMs learn to make high-quality experimental decisions from training practice, we perform fine-grained decision identification and quality annotation, then apply decision-level loss masking to exclude supervision on low-quality behavior. Experiments with GLM-5.3 on PostTrainBench, a benchmark for autonomous post-training, show that training on RSI-Prime yields a 31.95% relative improvement in overall performance over the original GLM-5.3, highlighting a path toward future RSI systems in which models autonomously sustain their own advancement.

## 2. Outcome-Balanced PPO and Policy-Induced Flow Rectification for Flow-Based VLA Policies

**Authors:** Yuhan Wang, Ruiyang Wang, Jiaguan Zhu
**arXiv:** [2611.22878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22878)
**Categories:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)

Reinforcement learning (RL) enables post-training of flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) policies by optimizing expected task return through environment interaction. However, this optimization raises two challenges: policy updates can alter the velocity-field geometry underlying the score approximation used for stochastic transitions, and sparse multi-task rollouts can yield uneven learning signals across tasks and advantage signs. We introduce an RL framework that combines Outcome-Balanced Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with policy-induced flow rectification. Outcome-Balanced PPO reweights positive advantages using task success-rate estimates and rescales positive and negative advantages while preserving their signs. Policy-induced rectification periodically fits a linear-bridge flow to ODE endpoint pairs generated by the current policy, refreshing the representation used in subsequent stochastic optimization. We evaluate the framework on simulated manipulation benchmarks and additionally study a component combination in a Real2Sim2Real task, examining task performance, optimization behavior, and flow consistency. The proposed framework improves success rates over the evaluated baselines across the simulated benchmarks.

## 3. Think Once, Act Fast: Cost-Effective Recursive Self-Improvement for Decision Model Harness

**Authors:** Siqi Lai, Haitao Li, Hao Liu
**arXiv:** [2611.22432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22432)
**Categories:** Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)

Fast System-1 models enable responsive actions but struggle with complex or unfamiliar tasks. Large language model (LLM)-based System-2 provides strong reasoning capabilities, yet repeated invocation incurs substantial latency and computational costs. To bridge this gap, we propose JevRSI, a System-1/System-2 collaboration framework that transforms LLM reasoning into transferable decision support through recursive harness improvement. A Jev-style System-1 model enables low-latency action selection, while an asynchronous System-2 LLM develops a modular harness. The harness integrates procedures, state memory, and world models to provide decision contexts spanning the past, present, and future. Utility-driven retrieval selects modules based on both contextual relevance and their utility on previous tasks. Environmental feedback guides targeted revisions and enables recursive self-improvement. Experiments across three datasets show that JevRSI consistently improves decision quality, matching or surpassing LLM agents in several settings. It preserves low-latency execution with favorable performance-cost trade-offs. Moreover, System-2 development interventions decrease as the harness evolves, amortizing System-2 costs through continuous improvement. Our project is available at this https URL.

## 4. Learning Agent-Centric Vision-Language-Action World Models for Autonomous Driving

**Authors:** Guoqing Wang, Pin Tang, Xiangxuan Ren, Chao Ma
**arXiv:** [2611.22880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22880)
**Categories:** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)

Recent progress in integrating vision-language-action (VLA) models with world modeling has significantly advanced end-to-end autonomous driving by allowing systems to reason about imagined futures instead of merely reacting to current observations. However, most world VLA models generate future frames at the pixel level with plenty of dense visual tokens. Although such generated futures can be visually plausible, they preserve many driving-irrelevant appearance details, i.e., only a small portion of the tokens directly encode critical planning-relevant factors, such as agent geometry, motion dynamics, and interactions. This introduces a significant performance bottleneck. An important question thus arises: what world state should a world VLA model reason over to support planning beyond pixel-level prediction? We argue that planning-oriented world modeling should focus on a compact and structured agent-centric latent state that captures the interactions between objects in the driving scene over time. As such, we introduce AgentWorld, an agent-centric VLA world model that uses discrete agent tokens jointly for reasoning and trajectory planning. Each token is dynamically grounded to a surrounding agent associated with its object's state, such as semantic identity, 3D geometry, motion, and interaction context. AgentWorld learns these tokens through three key processes: explicit geometric grounding, reasoning-oriented supervised fine-tuning, and agent-oriented reinforcement learning. Extensive open-loop and closed-loop experiments under diverse driving scenarios show that AgentWorld enhances planning capabilities, fully exploits visual evidence and agent dynamics, and yields interpretable agent-centric world states.

## 5. InteractVLA: Turning VLAs into Interactive Tools for Agentic Robotic Manipulation

**Authors:** Chen Zhao, Zipeng Ye, Shifeng Bao, ..., Yuchen Xie, Jing Zhang
**arXiv:** [2611.22584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22584)
**Categories:** Robotics (cs.RO)

Existing agentic manipulation systems treat pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies as opaque, language-only primitives: when a policy call fails under unfamiliar instructions or scene perturbations, the agent cannot steer its behavior and is forced into futile retries or costly manual takeover. We present InteractVLA, a framework that turns pretrained flow-based VLAs into geometrically steerable and inspectable tools through a bidirectional geometric interface with vision-language agents. In the forward direction, test-time geometric guidance translates an agent-specified end-effector target into input gradients through the action expert via a differentiable command-to-pose mapping, while soft-time target alignment treats arrival time as a latent execution index to match the target pose without distorting the policy's approach motion. In the reverse direction, action-informed agentic refinement exposes generated action chunks and calibrated camera reprojections back to the agent, allowing it to diagnose spatial discrepancies, revise targets for subsequent VLA calls, or locally adjust motion segments on top of the guided trajectory. Making the VLA controllable not only boosts online agentic tool use, but also unlocks effective recursive self-improvement (RSI), achieving state-of-the-art performance of 99.4\% on LIBERO-Plus, 48.62\% on RoboDojo, and 89.4\% on RoboCasa365. ## 6. VoxelSBFL: Voxel-Level Spectrum-Based Failure Prediction for Vision-Language-Action Models

**Authors:** Arusa Kanwal, Pablo Valle, Paola Roggero, ..., Shaukat Ali, Aitor Arrieta
**arXiv:** [2611.22453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22453)
**Categories:** Robotics (cs.RO); Software Engineering (cs.SE)

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models integrate visual perception, language understanding, and action generation to control robots. Testing these systems produces trajectories and task outcomes, but existing approaches provide limited guidance on using these recorded trajectories to assess subsequent executions or inform policy repair. We conjecture that, for a given policy and task, traversing certain workspace regions is associated with a higher likelihood of failure. To investigate this conjecture, we propose VoxelSBFL, a black-box adaptation of Spectrum-Based Fault Localization (SBFL) in which workspace voxels replace program elements as units of analysis. Recorded tool-center-point (TCP) trajectories provide binary voxel coverage, which is combined with execution outcomes to construct suspiciousness maps using established SBFL formulas. These maps identify failure-associated regions and score unseen trajectories without requiring internal model access. We evaluate 34 spectrum-based techniques across four VLA models using 96,000 Pick-Up trajectories in simulation and compare the leading techniques against eight action- and trajectory-based metrics. VoxelSBFL obtains an overall macro ROC-AUC of 0.704, significantly outperforming state-of-the art baselines. Sensitivity analyses show that performance varies with spatial granularity and training-set size. These findings support spatial spectra for VLA execution assessment and motivate future research on runtime monitoring, self-healing and targeted policy improvement.

## 7. SR-VLA: Stall-Reduced VLA Execution via Predicted-Input Lookahead and Selective KV Cache Repair

**Authors:** Tao Zhang, Yi Zhong, Jinhao Ruan, ..., Youming Yang, Yuan Wang
**arXiv:** [2611.22356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22356)
**Categories:** Robotics (cs.RO); Systems and Control (eess.SY)

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies incur control stalls when inference blocks the next action chunk. Asynchronous early inference can hide latency, but its results may be inconsistent with the arriving actual inputs. Unconditional reuse risks acting on mismatched inputs, whereas full recomputation discards useful precomputation. We propose SR-VLA, combining predicted-input lookahead with selective KV cache repair. During current chunk execution, a lightweight action-conditioned predictor generates next-boundary visual observations and proprioceptive state. These predictions, together with the task instruction, drive the frozen VLA to precompute a speculative KV cache and action chunk. After real inputs are available to VLA, predicted--real differences and task conditions determine whether to accept the action chunk or trigger selective KV repair. On LIBERO, SR-VLA achieves $2.67\times$ and $1.27\times$ query speedups over the respective synchronous $\pi_{0.5}$ and OpenVLA-OFT baselines, while maintaining competitive average task success rates. Under target-motion perturbation, success remains baseline-comparable, with $1.52\times$--$1.93\times$ query speedup.

## 8. VSpark: Speculative Decoding for Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action Models

**Authors:** Suho Yoo, Youngjoon Jang, Hyebin Cho, ..., Jaewook Jeong, Joon Son Chung
**arXiv:** [2611.22666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22666)
**Categories:** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV); Robotics (cs.RO)

Our aim is to accelerate autoregressive vision-language-action models (VLAs), which generate robot actions one token at a time. Speculative decoding makes this possible: a lightweight draft model proposes several tokens at once, and the VLA verifies them in a single pass, accepting only those that match its own predictions, so decoding remains lossless. The speedup, however, is limited in robot control, where many different tokens encode nearly the same motion and a draft close to the correct action is still rejected. To address this, our approach, VSpark, is built upon three design choices: (i) rollouts collected with calibrated action noise, which expose the draft to a wider range of robot states; (ii) feature alignment, which trains the draft to match the VLA's hidden features rather than its output tokens alone; and (iii) continuation reuse, which keeps the predictions the VLA has already computed along rejected drafts as candidates for the next verification step, instead of discarding them. On four VLAs with different action tokenizers and four manipulation benchmarks, VSpark achieves 1.28x to 2.10x speedup while keeping the VLA's decoding lossless.

## 9. CoBoVLA: Decoupling Language from Action with Discrete Codes for Robust and Interpretable Robot Manipulation

**Authors:** Mehul Arora, Dhruv Agarwal, Ambuj Agrawal
**arXiv:** [2611.22395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22395)
**Categories:** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV); Robotics (cs.RO)

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models achieve high success rates on the LIBERO benchmark but remain sensitive to paraphrasing. Existing VLAs ranging from 0.6B to 7.5B parameters experience a 22-52 point performance drop on LIBERO-Para while SmolVLA exhibits an even larger decline, from 83\% to 8\%. Because most VLAs jointly learn language understanding and action generation, it is difficult to determine whether a failure originates from misunderstanding an instruction or executing it incorrectly. To address these challenges, we introduce CoBoVLA, or CodeBookVLA, a 250M-parameter VLA that decouples language understanding, planning, and control through learned discrete interpretable codes. A text encoder maps the instructions to shared step codes, which a visual planner then converts into action code sequences executed by a language-independent actor. This design improves robustness to instruction paraphrasing while allowing failures to be traced to individual stages. It also enables efficient real-time control on resource-constrained hardware by encoding instructions only once and using compact discrete representations to keep each component lightweight. CoBoVLA achieves 91.4\% success on LIBERO-Goal, compared with 82.8\% for SmolVLA, under a matched protocol, with 17 ms per control step latency. On LIBERO-Para, it achieves 81.1\% across 4,092 unseen paraphrases and 78.5\% on matched episodes, compared with 36.5\% for SmolVLA LoRA fine-tuned on paraphrases. Without two-step demonstrations, CoBoVLA completes 48.8\% of 160 free-form two-step tasks. Finally, success reaches 91\% when instructions are correctly encoded but falls to 27.2\% otherwise, identifying language understanding as the main source of remaining failures. Together, these results demonstrate that decoupling language understanding and control can improve instruction robustness while making VLA failures easier to diagnose.

## 10. RefCloth: Transferable Adversarial Tablecloths for Vision-Language-Action Models

**Authors:** Shaozhen Liu, Zhiwei Li, Jiayang Sun, ..., Qi Li, Zhenan Sun
**arXiv:** [2611.22736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22736)
**Categories:** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)

Closed-loop vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely continuously on multi-view cameras, yet existing visual attacks typically hijack sensor streams with 2D patches or alter target object meshes that require retraining when manipulation objects change. We introduce RefCloth, a framework that optimizes one persistent adversarial tablecloth on a frozen source policy and deploys it zero-shot across unseen tasks and target models without modifying task objects or cameras. Throughout manipulation, static third-person and moving wrist views observe this shared world-bound texture under evolving occlusions. To generalize across shifting surface views while bridging the gap between differentiable and native renderers, RefCloth combines bounded texture parameterization with shared surface UV transformations and a same-path clean reference that removes static rendering offsets at initialization. Across four LIBERO suites in native simulation, one frozen texture transfers from OpenVLA-OFT to $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$, reducing success relative to an unoptimized cloth by 62.50 and 43.75 percentage points on average and far exceeding amplitude- and variation-matched random controls. Dual-view audits and target closed-loop camera interventions show that moving wrist exposure drives over twice the standalone drop of the main view with strong synergy, while ablations confirm the roles of UV diversification and path calibration.

## 11. HandRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement for Hand Pose Estimation

**Authors:** Zhekai Wang, Luqing Luo, Wenhao Yao, ..., Feifei Gao, Yuhua Jiang
**arXiv:** [2611.22164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22164)
**Categories:** Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)

Egocentric 3D hand pose estimators still miss hands at the image border, misplace occluded hands and jitter. Such failures are usually fixed by hand-written inference refinements and by fine-tuning, two interdependent steps that are tuned separately. We present HandRSI, a recursive self-improvement framework for a hand pose system that consists of a video estimator and its executable inference harness. In each round, coding agents refine the harness, and a change is accepted only if it improves a composite of all ten benchmark metrics on held-out data without significantly regressing any of them. The accepted harness then guides the estimator through selective distillation, which supervises hand presence and temporal consistency on frames without ground truth, while ground truth supervises pose. Since the updated estimator may change which refinements remain useful, the harness is re-validated on it. Starting from the released ACE-Ego-Hand model, five rounds reduce MPJPE-p by 35% on HOT3D and 11% on ARCTIC and roughly halve jitter. On HOT3D, HandRSI's MPJPE-p is 16% lower than that of ACE-Ego-Hand fine-tuned on the same ground truth. Under a unified protocol, it outperforms nine re-run published methods on all ten metrics of both benchmarks. Hypervolume selection on the accuracy-speed front yields Lite, Medium and Heavy systems running at 20.7 to 41.1 FPS. This report gives the full method, every stage of every round and the complete protocol. Code, evaluator, harnesses and weights are public.
